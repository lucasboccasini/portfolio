Prepare before
you perform.

A good run can start with a few calm minutes: check the conditions, get comfortable, warm up gradually, and choose a plan that fits today.

↗
Home / Before the run

Use the same simple sequence before an easy run, then adjust it for weather, route, and how you feel. The goal is to remove last-minute friction, not to complete a perfect ritual.

Your 30-minute pre-run routine

  1. 30 minutes out: check the forecast and choose layers that suit the conditions. If you want food, choose something familiar that sits comfortably for you.
  2. 15 minutes out: use the bathroom if needed, take a few sips of water, and check your route and return plan.
  3. 5–10 minutes out: begin with an easy walk, then move through comfortable dynamic movements.
  4. At the start: begin easier than you think you need to. Give yourself time to settle into the run.

A simple dynamic warm-up

Start with a few minutes of brisk walking or very easy jogging. Then choose movements that feel comfortable and controlled:

  • March in place or do gentle knee lifts.
  • Walk with easy leg swings, holding a wall or rail for balance if helpful.
  • Try a few ankle rolls and shoulder circles.
  • Finish with a relaxed walk or easy jog before picking up your planned pace.
Keep the warm-up easy.

A warm-up should prepare you for movement, not tire you out. Skip a movement that causes pain or feels unstable.

Gear and route checklist

  • Comfortable shoes and clothing for the conditions.
  • Phone, keys, and any safety items you need.
  • Route that matches your time, ability, and daylight.
  • Water access or a bottle if your route and conditions call for it.
  • A simple plan for turning back or shortening the run.

Food, water, and bathroom timing

There is no universal pre-run meal timing. Choose familiar foods, leave more time after a larger meal if that feels better, and note what works for you. Hydrate normally across the day; heat, humidity, and longer sessions may need extra planning.
