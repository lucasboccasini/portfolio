Fuel your
body.

Food and fluids are part of the bigger picture. Learn a few simple ways to fuel your day and find what feels comfortable before a run.

✳
Home / Nutrition

There is no single menu that fits every runner. Your needs depend on the length and effort of a run, your health, preferences, budget, and what foods are available. Start with regular meals and familiar foods rather than chasing a complicated plan.

Build a balanced everyday plate

For a practical starting point, include a source of carbohydrates, protein, and color across meals. Examples include:

  • Oats, rice, potatoes, bread, pasta, fruit, or other carbohydrate foods.
  • Beans, lentils, eggs, yogurt, fish, tofu, poultry, or other protein foods.
  • Vegetables and fruit you enjoy.
  • Fluids across the day, with more attention to conditions and effort when it is hot or humid.

Before your run

For a longer or harder session, a meal with familiar carbohydrate foods a few hours beforehand may feel useful. If you have less time, a small, familiar snack may sit better. Some people prefer a lighter stomach for an easy short run. Pay attention to comfort and experiment on ordinary training days.

Simple snack ideas

Try a banana, toast, applesauce, yogurt, or a small bowl of cereal if those foods work for you. You do not need a special product for every run.

After your run

Return to a normal meal or snack that includes carbohydrates and protein when it fits your day. Examples might be yogurt with fruit and granola, a sandwich and fruit, or rice with beans and vegetables. A regular meal can do the job; recovery does not need to be complicated.

Hydration basics

Drink regularly through the day and bring fluids when the length, weather, or route makes that useful. Needs vary with the person and the conditions. Avoid turning a general tip into a strict amount; a sports dietitian or healthcare professional can help tailor a plan, especially for long events or health conditions.

  • Check the forecast and consider heat and humidity.
  • Carry water for routes where it is needed or not available.
  • For long or hot sessions, follow qualified guidance about fluids and electrolytes.

Keep it practical

Build the habit before worrying about perfect timing.

  • Choose familiar foods.
  • Plan a snack for long class or work days.
  • Notice what feels comfortable.
  • Hydrate with the conditions in mind.
