Build your running
foundation.

Whether you are brand new or returning to running, start at a manageable level, progress gradually, and leave room for recovery.

⌁
Home / Running

There is no need to compare your first week with someone else’s tenth year. Pick a comfortable starting point and build a routine you can repeat. Walking breaks count, and rest is part of training.

Start with run-walk intervals

If continuous running feels like too much right now, alternate easy running with walking. One beginner example used by the NHS starts with a five-minute walking warm-up, short run intervals with walking recoveries, and a five-minute walking cool-down. Adjust the effort and duration to your own ability; you can also begin with walking alone.

Use the talk test.

For an easy effort, try a pace where you can speak in short sentences. Slow down or walk if you are struggling to talk comfortably.

A flexible beginner week

Here is a structure to adapt, not a prescription. Leave recovery time between running sessions, and repeat a week or session when you want more practice.

Build endurance with patience

  • Keep most beginner sessions comfortable and conversational.
  • Repeat a manageable session before adding more time or distance.
  • Change one part of the routine at a time and notice how you feel.
  • Keep rest days. Make an easy day easier when fatigue is building.
  • Choose a route and surface that feel safe and comfortable.

Form cues that stay simple

Look ahead, relax your shoulders, and let your arms swing naturally. Avoid forcing a particular foot strike or posture from a video. If a cue makes you tense or uncomfortable, drop it.

Rest and injury prevention

Some muscle soreness can happen when activity changes, but sharp, worsening, or persistent pain deserves attention. Stop if you feel unwell or experience concerning symptoms. If you have an injury or a condition that affects exercise, check in with a qualified healthcare professional for individual guidance.

Keep the base easy

Before increasing a challenge, ask:

  • Can I repeat this session?
  • Do I have recovery time?
  • Does this effort feel manageable today?
  • Am I enjoying the routine?
